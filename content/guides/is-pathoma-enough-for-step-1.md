## What Pathoma is

Pathoma is a pathology course by Dr. Husain Sattar. It has two parts that are meant to be used together:

- a short textbook, *Fundamentals of Pathology*, of a little over 200 pages in outline format;
- a set of video lectures, roughly 35 hours in total, that follow the book chapter by chapter.

The book is organized into 19 chapters. The first three cover general pathology: cell injury, inflammation and neoplasia. The rest go through the organ systems.

{{< book q="Pathoma Fundamentals of Pathology Sattar" title="Fundamentals of Pathology (Pathoma)" short="Fundamentals of Pathology" by="Husain A. Sattar" for="learning pathology mechanisms quickly before or during Step 1 preparation" >}}

One practical point before you buy: the videos are a subscription sold through the Pathoma website, and the textbook is included with it. A used copy of the book bought elsewhere does not come with video access.

## What it does well

Pathoma explains why diseases happen instead of listing facts to memorize. Once the mechanism is clear, the features of a disease follow from it, and there is less to remember. That is the reason it has stayed popular for more than a decade.

The first three chapters deserve special attention. General pathology underlies every organ system, and students widely treat those chapters as the part to know thoroughly.

## What it does not cover

Pathoma is a pathology resource, and Step 1 is not only a pathology exam.

| Step 1 area | Covered by Pathoma? |
|---|---|
| General and systemic pathology | Yes, this is its purpose |
| Pathophysiology of major diseases | Largely |
| Pharmacology | No |
| Microbiology | Only where it relates to disease processes |
| Biochemistry and genetics | Only in passing |
| Biostatistics and ethics | No |
| Practice questions | None |

The last row matters most. Step 1 tests whether you can apply knowledge to a clinical vignette, and Pathoma gives you no practice at that.

## Does pass/fail scoring change the answer?

Step 1 results have been reported as pass or fail only since January 26, 2022. That removed the pressure to chase a high three-digit score. It did not make the exam easy, and a concept-first resource like Pathoma suits a pass/fail exam well: the aim is solid understanding across the board, not rare details.

## What to use with it

1. **A question bank.** This is the part you cannot skip. Do questions by system as you finish the matching Pathoma chapter.
2. **A general review book** such as First Aid, to see everything the exam covers in one place. See [Is First Aid enough for Step 1?]({{< relref "is-first-aid-enough-for-step-1.md" >}}).
3. **A full textbook for gaps.** When a Pathoma explanation is too brief, look the topic up in Robbins. Our guide to [the two Robbins textbooks]({{< relref "robbins-basic-pathology-vs-pathologic-basis-of-disease.md" >}}) explains which to use.

## Is it worth the money?

If your school teaches pathology well and you already understand it, you may only need the first three chapters as a refresher. If pathology has been a weak subject, Pathoma is one of the more efficient ways to fix that.
