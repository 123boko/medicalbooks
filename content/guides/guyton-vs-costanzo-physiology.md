## The two books at a glance

| | Guyton and Hall | Costanzo |
|---|---|---|
| Full title | *Textbook of Medical Physiology* | *Physiology* |
| Role | Comprehensive reference | Concise teaching text |
| Length | Very long, covers every system in depth | Roughly half the length |
| Style | Detailed prose with clinical context | Step-by-step explanations, many worked problems |
| Best for | Courses built around it; looking things up | Learning concepts quickly; exam preparation |

## Who should choose Guyton and Hall

{{< book q="Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology" title="Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology" short="Guyton and Hall" by="Hall and Hall" for="courses built around it, and looking things up" >}}

{{< amazon q="Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology" text="Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology" >}} is the book many medical schools build their physiology course on. Its strength is completeness: when a lecture leaves a gap, the answer is almost always in there.

Choose it if:

- your lecturers assign readings from it;
- you prefer to read one topic in full depth instead of a summary;
- you want a single physiology reference for the rest of your degree.

The cost is time. Reading it cover to cover alongside a full course load is unrealistic for most students, so plan to read selected chapters.

## Who should choose Costanzo

{{< book q="Costanzo Physiology" title="Costanzo Physiology" short="Costanzo Physiology" by="Linda S. Costanzo" for="learning concepts quickly and exam preparation" >}}

{{< amazon q="Costanzo Physiology" text="Costanzo Physiology" >}} explains the same core material in far fewer pages. Each mechanism is built up in steps, and the end-of-chapter questions check whether you followed.

Choose it if:

- you find physiology hard and need the logic laid out plainly;
- you are short on time before exams;
- you are preparing for a licensing exam and want concepts, not exhaustive detail.

The same author also wrote {{< amazon q="BRS Physiology Costanzo" text="BRS Physiology" >}}, an outline-format review book. It is for revision, not first-time learning.

## What about Boron and Boulpaep?

{{< amazon q="Boron Boulpaep Medical Physiology" text="Medical Physiology by Boron and Boulpaep" >}} goes deeper into cellular and molecular mechanisms than either book above. It suits students who want that level of detail or who are heading toward research. For most pre-clinical courses it is more than you need.

## How to decide in two minutes

1. Check your course reading list. If one book is assigned, that settles it.
2. Borrow both from the library and read the same topic (the cardiac cycle works well) in each.
3. Buy the one you actually finished reading.

One main text read properly beats two read halfway.
