## Why the names cause confusion

Search for "Gray's Anatomy" in a bookshop and you get several different products: the big reference, the student textbook, an atlas, a review book, flashcards, and cheap reprints of the original nineteenth-century edition. They are not editions of one book. Buying the wrong one is a common and expensive mistake.

## Side by side

| | Gray's Anatomy | Gray's Anatomy for Students |
|---|---|---|
| Subtitle | *The Anatomical Basis of Clinical Practice* | none |
| Written for | Practising clinicians and anatomists | Medical and health science students |
| Authorship | Large team under an editor-in-chief | Drake, Vogl and Mitchell |
| Size | Very large single-volume reference | Standard course textbook |
| Organization | Detailed, by region and system, with development and microstructure | By region, each opening with a conceptual overview |
| How it is used | Looked up, not read through | Read alongside a course |

## Gray's Anatomy: the reference

{{< book q="Gray's Anatomy The Anatomical Basis of Clinical Practice" title="Gray's Anatomy: The Anatomical Basis of Clinical Practice" short="Gray's Anatomy" by="Standring, editor-in-chief" for="surgeons, radiologists and anyone who needs a definitive reference" >}}

This is the descendant of the book Henry Gray first published in 1858. It is written by many specialist contributors and aims to be complete. It covers variations, development and fine detail that no student exam asks about.

For a first-year student it is the wrong tool. It is too large to read, too detailed to revise from, and priced as a professional reference. Your library will have it for the rare occasion you need it.

## Gray's Anatomy for Students: the textbook

{{< book q="Gray's Anatomy for Students" title="Gray's Anatomy for Students" short="Gray's for Students" by="Drake, Vogl and Mitchell" for="medical students learning anatomy for the first time" >}}

This book was written as a teaching text. It is not an abridgement of the reference. It explains each region in stages, uses one consistent illustration style, and includes clinical cases and imaging at a level that matches course exams.

It competes with Moore's *Clinically Oriented Anatomy*, not with the big Gray's. See our [comparison of anatomy textbooks]({{< relref "best-anatomy-textbook-medical-students.md" >}}) for how those two differ.

## What about the cheap "Gray's Anatomy" reprints?

The low-priced hardbacks sold as gifts are reprints of very old editions that are out of copyright. They are interesting as history and have attractive engravings. They are not suitable for study: the terminology is dated and there is no modern imaging or clinical content.

## Which one should you buy?

- **Medical, dental or physiotherapy student:** Gray's Anatomy for Students.
- **Surgical trainee or radiology resident:** the reference, or access to it through your institution.
- **Buying a gift for someone starting medical school:** the student textbook is the useful one; check first that their course does not assign a different book.
