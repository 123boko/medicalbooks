## What First Aid actually is

*First Aid for the USMLE Step 1* is a review book published every year. It condenses the pre-clinical curriculum into tables, diagrams and short bullet points, organized by subject and organ system. It also includes mnemonics and a section rating other study resources.

{{< book q="First Aid for the USMLE Step 1" title="First Aid for the USMLE Step 1" short="First Aid Step 1" by="Le and Bhushan" for="a single-volume map of everything Step 1 covers" >}}

It was designed as a summary of material you have already learned. That design explains both its strength and its limit.

## Why it is not enough alone

**It tells you what, not why.** A typical entry gives the name of a disease, its cause and its key features in a few lines. If you have never understood the mechanism, the entry is just a list to memorize, and lists are easy to forget.

**It has no practice questions.** Step 1 questions are clinical vignettes. You read a short case and work out what is happening. Recognizing a fact in a table is a different skill from pulling it out of a patient story, and only practice questions train the second one.

**It is dense.** Every line is testable, which makes it tiring to read passively. Students who try to read it cover to cover as their main method often report that little of it stays.

## What it is good for

| Use | How well First Aid does it |
|---|---|
| Seeing the full scope of the exam | Very well |
| Quick lookup while doing questions | Very well |
| A place to add your own notes | Well; many students annotate it |
| Learning a topic for the first time | Poorly |
| Practising exam-style questions | Not at all |

## A workable combination

1. **Question bank as the core.** Do timed blocks and read every explanation, including for questions you got right.
2. **First Aid as the index.** After each block, find the topics you missed in First Aid and mark them.
3. **A teaching resource for weak areas.** For pathology, that is often Pathoma; see [Is Pathoma enough for Step 1?]({{< relref "is-pathoma-enough-for-step-1.md" >}}). For physiology, a short text such as Costanzo works; see [Guyton vs Costanzo]({{< relref "guyton-vs-costanzo-physiology.md" >}}).

## Does pass/fail change this?

Step 1 outcomes have been reported as pass or fail only since January 26, 2022. You no longer need to memorize the smallest details to gain a few points. You do still need to pass, and the passing standard requires broad, working knowledge. The combination above is still the reliable route; you can simply stop earlier.

## Which edition should you buy?

Buy the current one. First Aid is revised yearly to follow the exam and to correct errors reported by readers. This is one of the few books where we would not recommend saving money on an old copy; our guide to [older editions]({{< relref "older-edition-medical-textbooks.md" >}}) explains why review books are the exception.
